STATISTICS / HOMEWORK 05

Welford's Algorithm and Sampling Statistics

Building on Homework 4, we assign each of m hackers a sequence of n penetration scores from 0 to 10. We update the sample mean and variance one score at a time, then compare the estimates with theoretical values.

01 · WELFORD'S RECURRENCE

Updating statistics one score at a time

Knuth's recurrence updates the mean without storing previous scores. Welford extends the same idea to the sum of squared deviations, M₂, and the variance.

Mean updateδ = xₖ − μₖ₋₁μₖ = μₖ₋₁ + δ/k
Sum of squares updateM₂,ₖ = M₂,ₖ₋₁ + δ(xₖ − μₖ)s²ₖ = M₂,ₖ/(k−1), k > 1

Derivation, following the mean recurrence

Step 1 — Update the mean. Let δ = xₖ − μₖ₋₁. Knuth’s recurrence gives μₖ = μₖ₋₁ + δ/k.

Step 2 — Expand the centered sum of squares. Define SSₖ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ(xᵢ − μₖ)². For each old observation, xᵢ − μₖ = (xᵢ − μₖ₋₁) − δ/k; for the new one, xₖ − μₖ = δ(k−1)/k.

Step 3 — Simplify. Since Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ⁻¹(xᵢ − μₖ₋₁) = 0, the cross term vanishes. The old observations contribute SSₖ₋₁ + (k−1)δ²/k², while the new observation contributes (k−1)²δ²/k². Adding both contributions gives:

SSₖ = SSₖ₋₁ + (k−1)δ²/k = SSₖ₋₁ + δ(xₖ − μₖ)

Step 4 — Obtain the variance. The unbiased sampling variance is s²ₖ = SSₖ/(k−1) for k ≥ 2. Equivalently, s²ₖ = [(k−2)s²ₖ₋₁ + δ(xₖ − μₖ)]/(k−1) for k ≥ 3, with s²₂ = SS₂.

Initialize k = 0, mean = 0, M₂ = 0. M₂ is the centered sum of squares (SS), not Σxᵢ². The unbiased sample variance uses k−1 and is defined for k ≥ 2.

02 · THREE-LINE CORE

Welford in JavaScript

For each incoming score x, the recurrence itself needs just three statements (the count is updated separately):

const delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / count;
M2 += delta * (x - mean);

After all n scores: sample mean = mean; unbiased sample variance = M2/(n−1). This uses constant memory.

03 · SCORE DISTRIBUTION

Our fixed probability model

Each score is an independent integer chosen uniformly from 0 to 10 (inclusive). Every value has probability 1/11.

Possible scores

0, 1, 2, …, 10

Theoretical mean

μ = 5

Theoretical variance

σ² = 10

E[X] = (0+⋯+10)/11 = 5; Var(X) = E[X²] − 5² = 35 − 25 = 10.

Here variability is measured by the standard deviation (SD) of estimates across hackers. The theoretical SD of a sample mean is √(σ²/n) = √(10/n). The variability of sample variances also decreases as n increases, although it follows a different formula.

08 · DISCUSSION

What can we conclude?

Accuracy of the estimates

Across independent hackers, the average sample mean is close to μ = 5, and the average unbiased sample variance is close to σ² = 10. Both estimators are unbiased under this model (using n−1 for sample variance). Individual experimental averages can differ slightly from theory because they are random.

Increasing n

More systems per hacker generally reduce the variability of both sample means and sample variances. More hackers (m) make the empirical comparisons more reliable, without changing an individual hacker's estimator distribution.

In inferential statistics, sample estimates let us learn about an unknown population. Welford's recurrence computes them efficiently and stably as new observations arrive.
