STATISTICS / HOMEWORK 06

Numerical Integration and the Normal CDF

This homework explores numerical integration using Riemann and Lebesgue-inspired methods, and numerical computation of the standard normal CDF.

01 · TWO PERSPECTIVES ON INTEGRATION

Partition the domain or partition the range?

Riemann-inspired midpoint rule
∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx ≈ Δx · Σ f(a + (i + ½)Δx)

Divide the x-axis into N equal intervals, and add rectangles with midpoint heights.

Lebesgue-inspired range bins
∫ f dx ≈ Σ yⱼ · measure({x : f(x) in bin j})

Divide the y-values into K bins. Estimate each bin's preimage measure using a separate fine sampling of the x-domain.

Both methods approximate the same integral for ordinary integrable examples, but they group the information differently. Our second method is a numerical approximation inspired by Lebesgue's definition, not an exact computation of measurable sets. It needs domain samples to estimate the preimage sizes.

04 · PATHOLOGICAL EXAMPLES

When the methods behave differently

Dirichlet's function

f(x)=1 on rational numbers and 0 on irrational numbers. It is not Riemann-integrable: every interval contains both kinds of points, giving lower sums 0 and upper sums 1. Its Lebesgue integral is 0 because the rationals have measure zero.

A rational midpoint grid samples the value 1 at every point and misleadingly gives a sum of 1, even though the Lebesgue integral is 0. This illustrates a failure of naive sampling, not a valid Riemann integral.

Thomae's function

At a reduced rational p/q its value is 1/q; at irrational points it is 0 (take f(0)=1). It is Riemann-integrable and Lebesgue-integrable, with integral 0 under both definitions.

A rational grid shows spikes, but they become less influential as partitions refine. The exact positive-value set is countable, hence has measure zero. Rational-grid sample sums can be nonzero even though the exact integral is 0.

Why not simulate exact rationality with JavaScript?

Finite-precision numbers cannot tell whether an arbitrary real input is rational or irrational. Therefore the two pathological examples above are presented with their mathematical conclusions; the simulator does not invent an exact measure from floating-point samples. The Dirichlet rational-grid result of 1 is a counterexample to treating sampled estimates as proof of convergence.

07 · DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

What do these experiments tell us?

Riemann vs. Lebesgue

For Riemann-integrable functions, both approaches target the same integral. Riemann partitions x; Lebesgue organizes values y and measures their preimages. At finite resolution, our approximate methods can have different errors and computational costs.

Why do we need erf?

The Gaussian integral has no antiderivative expressible using elementary functions. Liouville's theorem on elementary antiderivatives provides the mathematical framework for proving this. Defining the special function erf gives an exact analytical representation of Φ, but computing its numerical values still requires algorithms.

Accuracy vs. work

Simpson's rule has high accuracy for the smooth normal density. The erf power series converges quickly for moderate |z|. The fast erf formula uses few arithmetic operations but sacrifices some accuracy. Choose an algorithm according to error tolerance and runtime.

Connection to earlier work

In Homework 4, the CLT compared normalized random scores with a standard normal distribution. Here we compute the cumulative probabilities under that distribution.

Numerical integration turns mathematical definitions into computable estimates. The normal CDF shows why a non-elementary integral can still be evaluated accurately and efficiently.
