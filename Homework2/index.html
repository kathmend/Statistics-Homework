A simple exploration of wallet key derivation, hashing, checksums and their statistical properties.

1. INTRODUCTION

From seed to address

An Ethereum wallet uses different cryptographic tools to transform a mnemonic phrase into a private key, a public key and finally an Ethereum address.

Each step has a different purpose: key derivation, elliptic-curve cryptography, hashing or error detection.

Statistical lab

Explore three useful ideas: entropy, bit balance, and the avalanche effect. Each experiment explains what it measures — and what it cannot prove.

EXPERIMENT A

How much entropy is in a mnemonic?

BIP-39 uses 11 bits per word index. Some bits are a checksum, not extra randomness.

5. DISCUSSION

Why not just one algorithm?

These techniques solve different problems. A checksum cannot replace a signature; a hash cannot organize a wallet's key tree.

CategoryStandard / primitivePurpose
Key generation & derivationBIP-39 / BIP-32 / BIP-44Represent initial randomness, derive reproducible keys and organize accounts with paths such as m/44'/60'/0'/0/0.
Public-key cryptographysecp256k1Derive a public key from a private scalar (Q = kG) and support digital signatures.
HashingKeccak-256Hash the public-key coordinates; Ethereum uses the last 20 bytes of the digest as its address.
Checksum & encodingEIP-55Use mixed-case hexadecimal letters to help detect address typing errors; this does not prove ownership.
Important distinction

Key generation creates fresh secret randomness; deterministic key derivation transforms existing secret material into repeatable keys. Hexadecimal and mnemonic words are encodings, not encryption.

6. HISTORICAL CONTEXT

How these ideas evolved

1977RSA

Public-key cryptography

2001SHA-2 / SHA-512

Standardized hash family

2012BIP-32

Hierarchical deterministic wallets

2013BIP-39

Mnemonic wallet proposal

2014BIP-44

Multi-account derivation paths

2015SHA-3

NIST standard based on Keccak

2016EIP-55

Mixed-case address checksum

SHA-512 ≠ Keccak-256 ≠ SHA3-256

SHA-512 belongs to SHA-2 and appears in BIP-39/BIP-32. Keccak is a different hash design that became the basis of SHA-3. Ethereum's Keccak-256 differs from standardized SHA3-256 in padding/domain separation.
