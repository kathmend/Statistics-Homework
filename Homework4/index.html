From systems to random trajectories

We consider n systems and m attackers. Each attacker encounters n systems and receives an independent score increment Xᵢ at every step. The cumulative score after n steps is Sₙ = Σ Xᵢ.

02 · SCORE MODELS

Bernoulli and Rademacher

Bernoulli

Xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}

Success adds 1; failure adds 0. The probability of success is p.

E[X] = p · Var(X) = p(1 − p)

Rademacher

Xᵢ ∈ {−1, +1}

Positive and negative increments are equally likely.

E[X] = 0 · Var(X) = 1

09 · DISCUSSION

Interpreting n systems and m attackers

Increasing n

For Bernoulli, the expected total is np. For Rademacher, the expected total stays at zero, while its variance grows with n. In both models, the average becomes more stable.

Increasing m

More attackers produce more independent trajectories and a more reliable empirical distribution. The distribution of a single attacker's score does not change. If scores are summed across all attackers, the aggregate expectation and variance scale with m.

The Law of Large Numbers explains why averages stabilize. The Central Limit Theorem explains why properly normalized Rademacher totals approach a bell-shaped distribution.
